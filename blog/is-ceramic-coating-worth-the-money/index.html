Is Ceramic Coating Worth the Money? Our Honest Opinion

Our honest opinion on whether ceramic coating is worth it, backed by manufacturer data, independent testing, and automotive publications.

Author: Henry Griffin | 19 September 2026

Short version: for the right car and the right owner, yes, we think it's worth it. For some people it isn't, and we'd rather tell you that up front than talk you into something that doesn't fit.

We're a veteran-owned, mostly mobile ceramic coating business, so we come to you. Obviously we have a stake in this answer, so we've tried to back this post with sources you can check yourself: automotive publications, manufacturer documents, and independent lab-style testing. Where they disagree with the sales pitch, I'll say so.

What ceramic coating actually is

A ceramic coating is a liquid polymer applied to clean, prepared paint. Motor Trend describes it as creating a semi-permanent bond with the paint, with silica (SiO2) as the main ingredient, in concentrations that range from about 10 to 85 percent depending on the product. Car and Driver makes a similar point: the concentration of SiO2 is the key differentiator between products, and professional-grade coatings are more concentrated and sold in small bottles.

Compared with wax, which Car and Driver says usually lasts a few months at most, a coating is built to last years. Our services page describes packages in year-tiers of approximately 2 to 3, 5 to 6, and 8 to 10 years. Those are approximate, manufacturer-style ratings, not promises: Car and Driver notes actual lifespan depends on prep, product quality, and how well the paint is maintained.

Why we think it's worth it

Here's our reasoning, in the order we'd weigh it.

1. Washing gets easier, and gentler on your paint

This is the benefit that holds up best. Motor Trend lists easier cleaning as a core benefit, because a coating helps keep dirt and water from clinging to the surface. Tom Voelk of Driven Car Reviews, who had a coating installed on his own Porsche Taycan, says it's "much easier to maintain than cleaning a car with bare paint." (Full disclosure from his video: STEK supplied the materials, and he promises to evaluate them honestly over time.)

Why does that matter for paint? In our experience, washing and drying are a leading source of swirl marks. The less you have to scrub, the fewer you add. That's the real argument for a coating, in our view: it's not armor, it's a slicker surface that makes careful washing easier.

2. Independent testing shows good coatings hold up to washing

Dmitry's Garage publishes ceramic coating rankings using instruments such as an elcometer abrasion test, a gloss meter, and water contact-angle measurements. In its 2025 list, the top coatings scored the maximum for wash durability, which the site defines as withstanding more than 1,000 washes in its abrasion test, and posted water contact angles above 100 degrees (the site says anything over 90 degrees counts as hydrophobic). One caution: that's a lab abrasion test on test panels, so it isn't a promise of years on your car. It tells you good coatings are durable against washing, not how long yours will last.

3. Preserving the gloss you paid for

Car and Driver lists high gloss and easier cleaning among the benefits, along with protection from water spots, bird droppings, and chemical stains. In our opinion, that matters most on dark paint, where every flaw shows. We can't give you a resale number, and we'd be wary of anyone who does. The publications above don't claim one either.

4. A real warranty exists at the pro level, with fine print

Manufacturers do stand behind some professional systems. XPEL's published warranty for Fusion Plus Premium V2, for example, runs eight years from installation, covers the paint finish against oxidation, loss of gloss, UV damage, and color fading, and only applies to professionally installed product. Read the conditions before you count on it: the warranty requires an annual inspection by a certified installer (which the owner pays for), excludes damage from improper wash technique and automatic car washes, doesn't cover water spots, and limits how much the company will pay toward a replacement. A warranty is a reason to prefer a certified installer, not a reason to skip maintenance. Whatever installer you choose, ask what the warranty covers and requires before you book. Our Ultimate package, for example, is installed to manufacturer standards with warranty registration when the system supports it.

Where we think the hype goes too far

Being honest here is part of why you can trust the rest.

  • It isn't scratch-proof or chip-proof. Car and Driver says coatings give a basic level of resistance to minor scratches but aren't bulletproof and don't completely protect against rock chips. Tom Voelk is blunter: a coating "offers zero impact resistance," unlike paint protection film. If you want chip protection, that's a different kind of product, and the video above explains the difference.
  • "9H" and "10H" are marketing numbers. Motor Trend notes coatings fall somewhere between 7H and 9H on the pencil hardness scale and are "far from bulletproof." Dmitry's Garage lists "coatings are as hard as glass or diamonds" and "coatings can stop scratches and chips" among the common marketing myths.
  • UV claims deserve skepticism. Manufacturers market UV protection, and some warranties cover UV damage. But Dmitry's Garage lists "a coating will block UV light" as a myth, and shows UV-A blocking as "Not Found" for its top two ranked coatings. So we don't sell on UV.
  • It isn't maintenance-free. You'll still wash it, and how you wash it affects how it performs. Dmitry's Garage calls "maintenance free" a myth, and Tom Voelk recommends a pH-balanced soap, two-bucket washing, and a clean towel or leaf blower to dry.
  • It doesn't fix existing damage. Swirls, haze, and oxidation stay visible under the coating. That's why we do paint correction first when it's needed.
  • Dealer add-ons are a different story. Consumer Reports advises against paying for dealer-added paint protection, saying modern factory paint is designed to last a decade or longer and periodic cleaning is all the car needs. That article is about dealership F&I add-ons in general and doesn't test ceramic coatings, but it's a fair counterpoint: your paint will not fall apart without a coating. Our case is easier washing and preserved appearance, not rescue from paint failure.

The value math, without fake numbers

We're not going to give you a neat savings figure, because that would be made up. Think about it this way:

  • Time. If washes go faster, that adds up over years. Only you can say what your time is worth.
  • Wax replacement. Wax and spray sealants need frequent reapplication. If you were already paying for regular protection treatments, compare that ongoing cost to a coating.
  • Appearance. If you keep a car for many years, or you care about how it looks when you sell, protecting the finish has real value, though not a number we can promise.
  • The cost itself. We don't publish prices. We quote after a paint check, by call or email. Vehicle size, paint condition, correction needed, coating tier and brand, time, and installer skill all play a part. See our post on how much ceramic coating costs for what drives the price.

What makes it more worthwhile in Northern Virginia

  • Road salt and brine. Winter road treatment clings to paint, wheels, and lower panels. A coated surface is easier to rinse clean.
  • Spring pollen. It's heavy and sticky. Hydrophobic paint makes rinsing simpler, and gentler rinsing means fewer swirl marks.
  • Humid summers and strong sun. Heat and UV are hard on clear coat over the years.
  • Water spots. Water that air-dries on paint can leave mineral spots. Car and Driver lists protection from water spots among the benefits of a coating, but a coating won't prevent them entirely.
  • Parking lots and tree sap. Between commuting on Route 7 and tight lots, cars pick up a lot of grime.

None of this means your paint falls apart without a coating. But if you want easier upkeep, a coating is one way to get it.

Who it's worth it for

It's probably worth it if you:

  • Just bought a new car or are leasing one and want to keep the paint looking good
  • Plan to keep your car for several years
  • Have dark paint that shows every flaw
  • Park outside, or don't have a garage
  • Like a clean car but don't love spending weekends washing it

It might not be worth it if you:

  • Plan to sell or trade the car soon and don't care about appearance
  • Are fine with a regular wax routine and enjoy doing it
  • Expect it to replace all maintenance, or to make the car scratch-proof
  • Drive an older car with serious paint failure, like peeling clear coat, that needs bodywork first

Why the installer matters

A coating is only as good as the prep and application. Skipping decontamination, rushing correction, or applying it in poor conditions (wind, dust, humidity swings) can hurt how well it bonds. When conditions or the job call for it, controlled-environment curing is an option. Frequently Asked Questions

Is ceramic coating worth it on a new car?

Often, yes. Protecting factory paint early means there's less to correct later. Even new cars usually benefit from at least a light polish first, according to Car and Driver.

Is ceramic coating worth it on an older car?

It can be, if the paint is in decent shape. If there's heavy oxidation or failing clear coat, correction or bodywork may come first, and a coating may not be the best use of money.

Does ceramic coating replace waxing?

For most people, yes. A coating is meant to do what wax does for much longer. You'll still wash the car regularly.

Will it stop swirl marks and scratches?

It helps by making the surface slicker and easier to wash gently, but it won't make paint scratch-proof.

Is it worth paying for the longer-lasting tier?

It depends on how long you'll keep the car and how much you value the extra longevity. We're happy to talk through it with you.
